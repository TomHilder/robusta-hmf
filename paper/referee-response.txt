EIGENSPECTRA, EXPLAINED VARIANCES, AND COEFFICIENT DISTRIBUTIONS

We fully agree that visualizations comparing eigenspectra, explained variances, and coefficient distributions across methods are essential for readers to understand how RHMF recovers underlying data structure without outlier contamination.

We have now added three new figures to Section 3 (Experiments):

1. Figure [eigenspectra]: Basis vector comparison across PCA, RPCA, RHMF, and ground truth for the toy dataset. Each row shows one of the first 5 basis functions (K1–K5), with each column representing a different method. The RHMF basis vectors recover the ground truth structure more cleanly than PCA, which is contaminated by outliers, while remaining computationally efficient compared to RPCA.

2. Figure [explained_variance]: Explained variance by component for PCA, RPCA, and RHMF on the toy dataset. The RHMF method allocates variance more cleanly across components than PCA, which wastes variance on fitting outliers. 3. Figure [coefficients]: Coefficient distributions for the toy dataset with histograms for PCA and RPCA (top and middle rows) and a scatter plot for RHMF colored by robust weights (bottom row). The RHMF scatter plot demonstrates how robust weights downweight outlier spectra (red/warm colors indicate low weights), providing a continuous measure of how anomalous each spectrum is in the space of each basis component.

These visualizations directly address the reviewer's request and provide readers with PCA-type comparisons essential for understanding RHMF's behavior. Extension to the Gaia dataset is feasible but computationally expensive; we recommend this for a future extended version once the full-dataset analysis (comment 9) is complete.

OUTLIER IDENTIFICATION: ROBUST WEIGHTS VS. COEFFICIENTS

We have added a new discussion subsection ("Complementary approaches to outlier identification") addressing this crucial point.

The key insight is that robust weights and coefficient-based detection are complementary mechanisms:

- Robust weights (w_ij) flag immediate deviations from the low-rank structure. A spectrum with low median weight is anomalous in the space of the K basis vectors.

- Higher-K coefficients reveal shared anomalous structure. If many outlier spectra share coherent deviations from the low-rank model, higher components capture that shared structure. High coefficients on these higher-K components can identify outliers that look similar to each other.

In practice, we recommend inspecting both mechanisms: robust weights identify outliers that violate the current model; higher-K coefficients identify whether those outliers share systematic structure. The choice of K should incorporate domain knowledge: larger K allows more outlier structure to be captured, at the risk of overfitting. Our cross-validation procedure (Section 2.4) addresses this trade-off.

SECTION 2: METHOD

3. Line 168 - What counts as outliers?

✓ ADDRESSED: We have added detailed clarification in the Method section explaining:

- Outliers are deviations from the low-rank structure, NOT class membership.
- Concrete example: 4 stellar types with populations (100, 100, 50, 10). The rare type with 10 spectra is NOT automatically "outliers" if they share coherent low-rank structure.
- A single anomalous spectrum IS an outlier even in a populous class.
- Model behavior depends on K choice and requires domain knowledge; this is why cross-validation over the (K, Q) grid is essential.

The discussion emphasizes that RHMF's behavior depends on assumptions about data structure, and users must verify these assumptions hold for their domain.

4. Line 4 - Mean subtraction in RHMF

✓ ADDRESSED: We have added explicit clarification after the Initialization section:

"Unlike PCA, RHMF does not require mean subtraction. The first basis vector can naturally capture the mean or offset of the data. This has practical advantages: we can apply RHMF directly to data without preprocessing, and we can represent datasets with different mean levels within a single basis."

This clarifies a subtle but important point that distinguishes RHMF from standard PCA implementations.

7. Line 368 and Figure 4 - KL vs. F1 divergence

✓ ADDRESSED: We have expanded the analysis of Figure 4 in the text to explain why best KL divergence and best F1 scores correspond to different (K, Q) values:

"The KL divergence and F1 scores optimize different objectives: KL divergence targets generalization (prediction error on held-out data), while F1 score targets detection accuracy (balance between true-positive and false-positive outlier identification). These different goals lead to different optimal hyperparameter choices. However, the parameter space is robust in the regime K≥5, Q≳3, indicating that the method is relatively insensitive to reasonable hyperparameter choices within this regime."
